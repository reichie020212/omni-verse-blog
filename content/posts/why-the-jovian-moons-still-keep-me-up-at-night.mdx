There is a particular kind of insomnia reserved for people who have read too much about Europa. You lie in bed and your brain, refusing to be useful, instead pictures a frozen shell over a dark salty ocean a hundred kilometers deep, and you start running probabilities.

## A neighborhood of impossibilities

Three of the four Galilean moons are doing something a textbook said they should not. Io is the most volcanically active body in the solar system; Europa is a snow globe with liquid; Ganymede is the only moon we know that generates its own magnetic field. They are siblings, sharing the same parent, and they could not be more different.

Whatever lives down there — if anything does — has been having a very long, very quiet conversation with itself in the dark.

## What Clipper might tell us

The Europa Clipper mission is in cruise. By the time it arrives, the data we have today will look quaint. I am okay with looking quaint about this. I would happily be wrong about every single thing.
